Imagine walking along the graph of \(f\) from \(x=a\) to \(x=b\) and recording only the vertical distance traveled, up or down. That total is the variation of \(f\). A function of bounded variation is one for which it is finite. Monotone functions have bounded variation, and so do functions with bounded derivatives. The main result, the Jordan Decomposition Theorem, says every function of bounded variation is a difference of two increasing functions. These functions are the natural integrators for the Riemann–Stieltjes integral in Section 9.6, and they return in Part III.

8.1 Partitions and Functions of Bounded Variation

Definition 8.1.1. A partition \(\mathcal{P}\) of \([a,b]\) is a finite collection \(\{x_0,x_1,\dots,x_n\}\) with \(a=x_0<x_1<\cdots<x_n=b\).

Definition 8.1.2. The mesh of a partition \(\mathcal{P}\) is defined as \(\left\lVert \mathcal{P}\right\rVert=\max_i\Delta x_i\), where \(\Delta x_i=x_i-x_{i-1}\).

Definition 8.1.3. A partition \(\mathcal{P}\) of \([a,b]\) is regular if \(x_k-x_{k-1}=\frac{b-a}{n}\) for all \(k=1,2,\dots,n\).

Definition 8.1.4. A partition \(\mathcal{P}^*\) of \([a,b]\) is a refinement of the partition \(\mathcal{P}\) if \(\mathcal{P}\subset\mathcal{P}^*\).

Definition 8.1.5. Suppose \(f:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}\). If \(\mathcal{P}=\{x_0,x_1,\dots,x_n\}\) is a partition of \([a,b]\), define \[V(\mathcal{P})=\sum_{k=1}^n|f(x_k)-f(x_{k-1})|.\] Define the total variation of \(f\) on \([a,b]\) by \[V_f[a,b]=\sup_{\mathcal{P}} V(\mathcal{P}),\] where the supremum is taken over all possible partitions of \([a,b]\). If \(V_f[a,b]<\infty\), \(f\) is of bounded variation on \([a,b]\). The set of functions of bounded variation on \([a,b]\) is denoted \(BV[a,b]\).

Here \(V_f[a,b]=\infty\) means the set of sums \(V(\mathcal{P})\) is not bounded above, as in Remark 3.6.7. When more than one function or interval is involved, the context says which one \(V(\mathcal{P})\) refers to.

Remark 8.1.6. Notice that by picking \(\mathcal{P}=\{a,b\}\), \(V(\mathcal{P})=|f(b)-f(a)|\). So \(V_f[a,b]\ge |f(b)-f(a)|\).

Lemma 8.1.7. Let \(f:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}\). If \(\mathcal{P}^*\) is a refinement of the partition \(\mathcal{P}\) of \([a,b]\), then \(V(\mathcal{P})\le V(\mathcal{P}^*)\).

Proof. First suppose \(\mathcal{P}^*=\mathcal{P}\cup\{x^*\}\) has one more point than \(\mathcal{P}=\{x_0,\dots,x_n\}\). Then \(x_{k-1}<x^*<x_k\) for some \(k\). By the Triangle Inequality, \[|f(x_k)-f(x_{k-1})|\le|f(x_k)-f(x^*)|+|f(x^*)-f(x_{k-1})|.\] The left side is the term of \(V(\mathcal{P})\) for \([x_{k-1},x_k]\). The right side is the sum of the two terms of \(V(\mathcal{P}^*)\) that replace it. All other terms are the same. So \(V(\mathcal{P})\le V(\mathcal{P}^*)\). In general, \(\mathcal{P}^*\) has finitely many more points than \(\mathcal{P}\). Adding them one at a time gives the result.

∎

Proof Technique (Telescoping Sums).

A sum of the form \(\sum_{k=1}^n(a_k-a_{k-1})\) collapses. Writing it out, \((a_1-a_0)+(a_2-a_1)+\cdots+(a_n-a_{n-1})\), every term cancels except \(a_n-a_0\). Such a sum is called telescoping. The proof of Theorem 8.1.8 uses it with \(a_k=f(x_k)\). The proof of Theorem 8.1.9 uses it with \(a_k=x_k\), which gives \(\sum_{k=1}^n\Delta x_k=b-a\) for every partition of \([a,b]\).

Theorem 8.1.8. Let \(f:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}\) be monotone on \([a,b]\). Then \(f\) is of bounded variation on \([a,b]\) with \(V_f[a,b]=|f(b)-f(a)|\).

Proof. Assume without loss of generality that \(f\) is increasing on \([a,b]\). (Otherwise, replace \(f\) by \(-f\), which has the same variation.) Let \(\mathcal{P}=\{x_0,x_1,\dots,x_n\}\) be a partition of \([a,b]\). Then \[\begin{aligned} \sum^n_{k=1}|f(x_k)-f(x_{k-1})|&=\sum^n_{k=1}\bigl(f(x_k)-f(x_{k-1})\bigr)\\ &=f(x_1)-f(x_0)+f(x_2)-f(x_1)+\cdots+f(x_n)-f(x_{n-1})\\ &=f(x_n)-f(x_0)\\ &=f(b)-f(a). \end{aligned}\] The first equality holds since \(f(x_k)\ge f(x_{k-1})\) for each \(k\). So every sum \(V(\mathcal{P})\) equals \(f(b)-f(a)\). So \(V_f[a,b]=f(b)-f(a)\).

∎

Theorem 8.1.9. Let \(f:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}\) be differentiable on \([a,b]\) with \(f'\) bounded on \([a,b]\). Then \(f\) is of bounded variation on \([a,b]\).

Proof. Since \(f'\) is bounded on \([a,b]\), there exists an \(M>0\) such that \(|f'(x)|\le M\) for all \(x\in[a,b]\). Let \(\mathcal{P}=\{x_0,x_1,\dots,x_n\}\) be a partition of \([a,b]\). By the Mean Value Theorem, for each \(k=1,2,\dots,n\), there exists a \(c_k\in(x_{k-1},x_k)\) such that \[f(x_k)-f(x_{k-1})=f'(c_k)(x_k-x_{k-1}).\] So \[|f(x_k)-f(x_{k-1})|=|f'(c_k)||x_k-x_{k-1}|\le M|x_k-x_{k-1}|.\] This implies \[\sum^n_{k=1}|f(x_k)-f(x_{k-1})|\le\sum^n_{k=1}M|x_k-x_{k-1}|=M(b-a).\] Hence \(V_f[a,b]\le M(b-a)\) and the function \(f\) is of bounded variation on \([a,b]\).

∎

Corollary 8.1.10. Let \(f\in C^{(1)}[a,b]\). Then \(f\) is of bounded variation on \([a,b]\).

Proof.

By Theorem 6.3.14, \(f'\) is bounded on \([a,b]\). So Theorem 8.1.9 applies.

∎

Example 8.1.11. Notice that \(f:[-1,1]\to\mathbb{R}\), \(f(x)=\sqrt[3]{x}\), is of bounded variation on \([-1,1]\) since it is strictly increasing on \([-1,1]\). However, its derivative is unbounded on \([-1,1]\setminus\{0\}\), and \(f'(0)\) does not exist. So the converse of Theorem 8.1.9 is false. (For \(x<0\), \(\sqrt[3]{x}=-\sqrt[3]{-x}\).)

Theorem 8.1.12. Let \(f:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}\) satisfy a Lipschitz condition on \([a,b]\). Then \(f\) is of bounded variation on \([a,b]\).

Proof. See Exercise 8.1.2.

∎

8.2 Jordan Decomposition Theorem

This section shows that a function of bounded variation is the difference of two increasing functions. The two functions are built from the variation of \(f\) on \([a,x]\), viewed as a function of \(x\).

Theorem 8.2.1. Let \(f:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}\). If \(f\) is of bounded variation on \([a,b]\) and \(c\in(a,b)\), then \(f\) is of bounded variation on \([a,c]\) and \([c,b]\), and \(V_f[a,b]=V_f[a,c]+V_f[c,b]\).

Proof. Let \(\mathcal{P}_a=\{x_0,x_1,\dots,x_j\}\) be a partition of \([a,c]\) and let \(\mathcal{P}_b=\{y_0,y_1,\dots,y_n\}\) be a partition of \([c,b]\). Then \(\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{P}_a\cup\mathcal{P}_b\) is a partition of \([a,b]\) with \[V(\mathcal{P})=\sum^j_{k=1}|f(x_k)-f(x_{k-1})|+\sum^n_{k=1}|f(y_k)-f(y_{k-1})|\le\sup_{\mathcal{P}} V(\mathcal{P})=V_f[a,b].\] Take the supremum over all partitions of \([a,c]\). Then \[V_f[a,c]+\sum^n_{k=1}|f(y_k)-f(y_{k-1})|\le \sup_{\mathcal{P}} V(\mathcal{P})=V_f[a,b].\] So \(f\) is of bounded variation on \([a,c]\). Then taking the supremum over all partitions of \([c,b]\), \[V_f[a,c]+V_f[c,b]\le \sup_{\mathcal{P}} V(\mathcal{P})=V_f[a,b].\] So \(f\) is of bounded variation on \([c,b]\).

Now let \(\mathcal{P}=\{x_0,\dots,x_n\}\) be a partition of \([a,b]\). Without loss of generality assume \(c\in\mathcal{P}\). If not, add it. By Lemma 8.1.7, this does not decrease \(V(\mathcal{P})\). So there exists a \(k\in\{1,\dots,n\}\) with \(c=x_k\). Then \(\mathcal{P}_a=\{x_0,\dots,x_k\}\) is a partition of \([a,c]\) and \(\mathcal{P}_b=\{x_k,\dots,x_n\}\) is a partition of \([c,b]\). Now \[V(\mathcal{P})=V(\mathcal{P}_a)+V(\mathcal{P}_b)\le V_f[a,c]+V_f[c,b].\] By taking the supremum of all partitions of \([a,b]\), \[V_f[a,b]\le V_f[a,c]+V_f[c,b].\] So \[V_f[a,b]= V_f[a,c]+V_f[c,b].\]

∎

Proof Technique (Taking Suprema One at a Time).

The first half of the proof of Theorem 8.2.1 has two independent choices, a partition \(\mathcal{P}_a\) of \([a,c]\) and a partition \(\mathcal{P}_b\) of \([c,b]\). It knows \(V(\mathcal{P}_a)+V(\mathcal{P}_b)\le V_f[a,b]\) for every pair. To get \(V_f[a,c]+V_f[c,b]\le V_f[a,b]\), it fixes \(\mathcal{P}_b\) and takes the supremum over \(\mathcal{P}_a\). The result still holds for every \(\mathcal{P}_b\), so it then takes the supremum over \(\mathcal{P}_b\). Each step uses only that a supremum is the least upper bound. If every element of a set is at most \(K\), then so is the supremum.

Theorem 8.2.2. Suppose that \(f:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}\) is of bounded variation on \([a,b]\). Define \(V_f(x)=V_f[a,x]\) for \(a<x\le b\), and \(V_f(a)=0\). Then the function \(V_f\) is increasing on \([a,b]\).

Proof. By Theorem 8.2.1, \(V_f\) is a well-defined function. Now let \(x_1,x_2\in[a,b]\) with \(x_1<x_2\). If \(x_1=a\), then \(V_f(x_1)=0\le V_f(x_2)\). So assume \(x_1>a\). By Theorem 8.2.1 on \([a,x_2]\), \[V_f[a,x_2]=V_f[a,x_1]+V_f[x_1,x_2].\] So \[V_f(x_2)-V_f(x_1)=V_f[x_1,x_2]\ge 0.\] Thus, \(V_f(x_1)\le V_f(x_2)\) and \(V_f\) is increasing on \([a,b]\).

∎

Theorem 8.2.3. Suppose that \(f:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}\) is of bounded variation on \([a,b]\). Then \(f\) is continuous at \(c\in[a,b]\) if and only if \(V_f\) is continuous at \(c\).

Proof.

(\(\Leftarrow\)) Let \(x\in[a,b]\). If \(c<x\), then by Remark 8.1.6 and Theorem 8.2.1, \[|f(x)-f(c)|\le V_f[c,x]=V_f(x)-V_f(c).\] If \(x<c\), the same argument gives \(|f(c)-f(x)|\le V_f(c)-V_f(x)\). So \(|f(x)-f(c)|\le|V_f(x)-V_f(c)|\) for all \(x\in[a,b]\). Let \(\varepsilon>0\). Since \(V_f\) is continuous at \(c\), there exists a \(\delta>0\) such that \(|V_f(x)-V_f(c)|<\varepsilon\) for all \(x\in[a,b]\) with \(|x-c|<\delta\). For these \(x\), \(|f(x)-f(c)|<\varepsilon\). So \(f\) is continuous at \(c\).

(\(\Rightarrow\)) Suppose \(f\) is continuous at \(c\). First suppose \(c<b\). To show \(\lim_{x\to c^+}V_f(x)=V_f(c)\), let \(\varepsilon>0\). Since \(f\) is continuous at \(c\), there exists a \(\delta>0\) such that \(|f(x)-f(c)|<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\) for all \(x\in[a,b]\) with \(|x-c|<\delta\). Since \(V_f[c,b]-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\) is less than the supremum \(V_f[c,b]\), there exists a partition \(\mathcal{P}=\{x_0,x_1,\dots,x_n\}\) of \([c,b]\) with \(V(\mathcal{P})>V_f[c,b]-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\). By Lemma 8.1.7, adding points to \(\mathcal{P}\) does not decrease \(V(\mathcal{P})\). So assume \(x_1-c<\delta\) and \(x_1<b\). Then \[V_f[c,b]-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}<V(\mathcal{P})=|f(x_1)-f(c)|+\sum_{k=2}^n|f(x_k)-f(x_{k-1})|<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}+V_f[x_1,b].\] By Theorem 8.2.1, \(V_f[c,b]-V_f[x_1,b]=V_f[c,x_1]\). So \(V_f[c,x_1]<\varepsilon\). Now let \(c<x<x_1\). Since \(V_f\) is increasing, \[0\le V_f(x)-V_f(c)=V_f[c,x]\le V_f[c,x_1]<\varepsilon,\] where the last step uses Theorem 8.2.2 on \([c,b]\). So \(|V_f(x)-V_f(c)|<\varepsilon\) for all \(x\) with \(c<x<x_1\). Thus \(\lim_{x\to c^+}V_f(x)=V_f(c)\).

If \(c>a\), a similar argument with a partition of \([a,c]\) shows \(\lim_{x\to c^-}V_f(x)=V_f(c)\). Together, these show \(V_f\) is continuous at \(c\). (At \(c=a\) only the right-hand limit is needed, and at \(c=b\) only the left-hand limit.)

∎

Theorem 8.2.4. Suppose that \(f:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}\) is of bounded variation on \([a,b]\). Then the function \(V_f-f\) is increasing on \([a,b]\).

Proof. Let \(x_1,x_2\in[a,b]\) with \(x_1<x_2\). Then \[\begin{aligned} (V_f-f)(x_2)-(V_f-f)(x_1)&=V_f(x_2)-V_f(x_1)-(f(x_2)-f(x_1))\\ &=V_f[x_1,x_2]-(f(x_2)-f(x_1))\\ &\ge |f(x_2)-f(x_1)|-(f(x_2)-f(x_1))\\ &\ge 0. \end{aligned}\] The first inequality is Remark 8.1.6 on \([x_1,x_2]\). Thus \((V_f-f)(x_2)\ge(V_f-f)(x_1)\) and \(V_f-f\) is increasing on \([a,b]\).

∎

Theorem 8.2.5 (Jordan Decomposition Theorem).

Suppose that \(f:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}\). Then \(f\) is of bounded variation on \([a,b]\) if and only if \(f\) can be written as the difference of two increasing functions.

Proof. (\(\Leftarrow\)) Suppose that \(g,h:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}\) are increasing functions and \(f\) is defined by \(f=g-h\). By Theorem 8.1.8, \(g\) and \(h\) are of bounded variation with \(V_g[a,b]=g(b)-g(a)\) and \(V_h[a,b]=h(b)-h(a)\). Let \(\mathcal{P}=\{x_0,\dots,x_n\}\) be a partition of \([a,b]\). Then \[\begin{aligned} V(\mathcal{P})&=\sum_{k=1}^n|f(x_k)-f(x_{k-1})|\\ &=\sum_{k=1}^n|g(x_k)-g(x_{k-1})-(h(x_k)-h(x_{k-1}))|\\ &\le \sum_{k=1}^n|g(x_k)-g(x_{k-1})|+\sum_{k=1}^n|h(x_k)-h(x_{k-1})|\\ &=g(b)-g(a)+h(b)-h(a). \end{aligned}\] So \(V_f[a,b]\le g(b)-g(a)+h(b)-h(a)<\infty\) and \(f\) is of bounded variation on \([a,b]\).

(\(\Rightarrow\)) If \(f\) is of bounded variation on \([a,b]\), then by Theorems 8.2.2 and 8.2.4, \(V_f\) and \(V_f-f\) are increasing functions. But \(f=V_f-(V_f-f)\). So \(f\) is the difference of two increasing functions.

∎
