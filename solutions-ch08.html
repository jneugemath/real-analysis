Solutions for Chapter 8. Functions of Bounded Variation

Exercise 8.1.1. Hint. Show that \(|f'(x)|\le\alpha+\beta\) for \(0<x\le1\) and that \(|f(x)|\le x\). Then \(f\) satisfies a Lipschitz condition, by the Mean Value Theorem. Use Theorem 8.1.12.

Exercise 8.1.2. Hint. Each term of a variation sum satisfies \(|f(x_i)-f(x_{i-1})|\le M(x_i-x_{i-1})\). These bounds add up to \(M(b-a)\).

Solution. Choose \(M>0\) with \(|f(x)-f(y)|\le M|x-y|\) for all \(x,y\in[a,b]\). Let \(\mathcal{P}=\{x_0,\dots,x_n\}\) be a partition of \([a,b]\). Then \[V(\mathcal{P})=\sum_{k=1}^n|f(x_k)-f(x_{k-1})|\le\sum_{k=1}^nM(x_k-x_{k-1})=M(b-a),\] since the last sum telescopes. So \(M(b-a)\) is an upper bound for the sums \(V(\mathcal{P})\), and \(V_f[a,b]\le M(b-a)<\infty\). So \(f\) is of bounded variation on \([a,b]\).

Exercise 8.1.3. Hint. Use the partition \(\{a,x,b\}\).

Solution. Let \(x\in[a,b]\). If \(x=a\), then \(|f(x)-f(a)|=0\le V_f[a,b]\). If \(x=b\), then \(|f(x)-f(a)|\le V_f[a,b]\) by Remark 8.1.6. If \(a<x<b\), then \(\{a,x,b\}\) is a partition of \([a,b]\). So \[|f(x)-f(a)|\le|f(x)-f(a)|+|f(b)-f(x)|=V(\{a,x,b\})\le V_f[a,b].\] In every case, \(|f(x)-f(a)|\le V_f[a,b]\). By the Triangle Inequality, \[|f(x)|\le|f(a)|+|f(x)-f(a)|\le|f(a)|+V_f[a,b].\] Since \(V_f[a,b]<\infty\), this is one bound for every \(x\in[a,b]\). So \(f\) is bounded.

Exercise 8.1.4. Hint. Use the points \(\frac{2}{(2k+1)\pi}\). You may use that the sums \(1+\frac12+\cdots+\frac1n\) are not bounded. To see this, group the terms \(\frac13+\frac14\), then \(\frac15+\cdots+\frac18\), and so on.

Exercise 8.1.5. Hint. Choose a partition whose points alternate between rational and irrational numbers. Each term of its variation sum is \(1\).

Exercise 8.2.1. Hint. \(f\) decreases on \([-1,0]\) and increases on \([0,1]\). On each piece the variation is the total change in \(f\).

Solution. Let \(f(x)=x^2\). Since \(f\in C^{(1)}[-1,1]\), \(f\) is of bounded variation on \([-1,1]\) by Corollary 8.1.10. By definition, \(V_f(-1)=0\).

Let \(-1<x\le0\). Then \(f\) is decreasing on \([-1,x]\). By Theorem 8.1.8, \[V_f(x)=V_f[-1,x]=|f(x)-f(-1)|=1-x^2.\] Let \(0<x\le1\). By Theorem 8.2.1 on \([-1,x]\) with \(c=0\), and Theorem 8.1.8 on \([-1,0]\) and \([0,x]\), \[V_f(x)=V_f[-1,0]+V_f[0,x]=1+x^2.\] So \[V_f(x)=\begin{cases}1-x^2, & -1\le x\le0,\\ 1+x^2, & 0<x\le1,\end{cases}\qquad V_f(x)-f(x)=\begin{cases}1-2x^2, & -1\le x\le0,\\ 1, & 0<x\le1.\end{cases}\] Both functions are increasing on \([-1,1]\), by Theorems 8.2.2 and 8.2.4. This can also be checked directly. Then \[x^2=V_f(x)-\bigl(V_f(x)-f(x)\bigr)\] writes \(f\) as the difference of two increasing functions.

Exercise 8.2.2. Let \(h=f+g\) or \(h=f-g\). Let \(\mathcal{P}=\{x_0,\dots,x_n\}\) be a partition of \([a,b]\). By the Triangle Inequality, for each \(k\), \[|h(x_k)-h(x_{k-1})|\le|f(x_k)-f(x_{k-1})|+|g(x_k)-g(x_{k-1})|.\] Adding over \(k\), the sum \(V(\mathcal{P})\) for \(h\) is at most the sum \(V(\mathcal{P})\) for \(f\) plus the sum \(V(\mathcal{P})\) for \(g\). So it is at most \(V_f[a,b]+V_g[a,b]\). This holds for every partition. So \[V_h[a,b]\le V_f[a,b]+V_g[a,b]<\infty,\] and \(h\) is of bounded variation on \([a,b]\).

Exercise 8.2.3. Hint. Use Exercise 8.1.3.

Exercise 8.2.4. Hint. Write \(f=V_f-(V_f-f)\). Use Theorems 8.2.3 and 8.2.4.
